A strong GWAS signal and a weak loss-of-function result need not disagree. The Spence Lab studies why these tests reveal different aspects of a trait. But what can a weak result actually rule out?

I built a way to separate tightly bounded gene effects from effects that remain uncertain. Explore the lab’s public estimates, change the effect size you care about, and see which follow-up questions the evidence can support.

How it works

Each line below is an uncertainty interval for a gene’s estimated loss-of-function burden effect. The shaded band is a range you choose around zero. A line inside that band tells a different story from a line that extends far beyond it, even when neither estimate is significant.

These are public UK Biobank summary estimates deposited with Specificity, length and luck drive gene rankings in association studies. Effects use inverse-rank-normalized trait units, not clinical units. A gene’s presence in a GWAS region does not make it causal.

A small estimate is not always a precise estimate.

Choose a trait and region. Move the threshold to ask whether the interval bounds a small effect, supports a larger one, or leaves the question open.

Interval assumptions

These normal-approximation intervals are descriptive. Rare-carrier estimates may depart from this approximation. A threshold is a sensitivity choice, not a validated biological cutoff or a formal equivalence test.

Try:
—within the range
—entirely beyond it
—unresolved

What this lets the lab do: distinguish imprecision from a tightly bounded effect before treating GWAS–burden disagreement as a biological clue.

Where I could add value: make the assumptions, missing estimates and sensitivity of that distinction inspectable, with an export that can be taken back to a research pipeline.

Why this matters for the lab

The paper explains why GWAS and loss-of-function rankings need not identify the same genes. This companion analysis asks a narrower follow-up: when is an apparently weak burden result sufficiently precise to make that contrast worth investigating?

A wide interval points first toward carrier counts, variant annotation and independent replication. A tightly bounded effect at a strong GWAS region motivates fine-mapping and colocalization before comparing context-specific regulation with gene-level dosage. Neither path follows from proximity alone, and a small burden effect does not imply that a gene is unimportant.

What I would bring

My pancreatic cancer work connected computational analysis to biological questions. I would like to bring that habit to genetic data: start with an explicit question, check how the inputs and assumptions affect the answer, and make the result easy for others to examine and build on.

This is an assistant-supported exploratory project. Its reproducible analysis and tests demonstrate a proposed way of working, not prior independent experience developing population-genetics methods.

Methods note

For effect estimate β and standard error SE, the interval is β ± z × SE. “Within” means the whole interval lies in [−δ, δ]; “beyond” means the whole interval lies above δ or below −δ; all other valid intervals are “unresolved.” Missing or ambiguous gene mappings are not classified. No genes are filtered by burden significance.

Sources, coverage and limitations

These are selected leading GWAS regions, not a representative set of all genes. The displayed gene–region entries are not independent samples. The analysis does not identify causal genes, estimate clinical risk, establish equivalence or reproduce the paper’s complete pipeline.
